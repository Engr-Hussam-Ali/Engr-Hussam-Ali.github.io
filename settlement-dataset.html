A verified settlement dataset for placing sensors in buildings

An automated Abaqus pipeline that builds 108 concrete-frame buildings, runs 10,692 settlement analyses and checks every result, packaged as a Windows program. Safety IoT project of KRISS, South Korea's national measurement standards laboratory, at Sejong University.

In short

  • 10,692 analyses over 108 buildings solved without a failure.
  • Every building passed eight physical and numerical gates and a superposition check (residual ≤ 6.6×10−7).
  • Mesh refinement changes the candidate tilts by at most 0.789 % and leaves their ranking unchanged (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.99975).
  • The result is a 432-file training dataset and an installable program that runs the whole pipeline from a GUI.

Why a dataset

When the ground under a building settles unevenly, columns and joints rotate. A tilt sensor reports the settlement only if it sits where that rotation is large, and where that is depends on the building and on the settlement pattern. The project's plan is to let a graph neural network choose the location. That needs a large, verified set of simulations first, and building that set was my job.

The pipeline

Eight steps, each writing its own log, run end to end without manual editing of models:

  1. Building parametersPlan grid, storeys, spans, storey heights, setbacks.
  2. Building catalogue108 buildings, with member sizes from design rules.
  3. Model generationAbaqus input decks written by script.
  4. Gravity analysis and gatesEach building must pass eight checks.
  5. Settlement scenariosTwo complementary methods, A and B.
  6. SolvingBatch runs on the licence pool.
  7. Result extractionDisplacements and rotations at every node.
  8. Dataset432 files, four per building.

Building model

  • Low-rise (one to three storeys) reinforced-concrete frames: beam elements for columns and beams, shells for slabs and footing pads. Every column node is a candidate sensor location.
  • Footings rest on Winkler springs (ks = 4.0×107 N/m³ with Terzaghi's size correction) with no vertical support, so the soil carries all vertical load.
  • Concrete fck 24 MPa, Ec 25.8 GPa, ν 0.20, with cracked-section stiffness factors of 0.70 for columns, 0.35 for beams and 0.25 for slabs, following the Korean design standards (KDS).
  • Service loads (1.0 dead + 1.0 live), linear static: self-weight, superimposed dead load, live load and backfill on the pads, taken from the KDS load tables.

108 buildings

Nine plan grids from 3×3 to 5×5 columns, one to three storeys, spans of 4.5 to 7.5 m and storey heights of 3.0 to 4.0 m; 52 buildings have setbacks. Members are sized with design rules of thumb; the models make no claim of code compliance. In total: 1,728 footings, 669,971 nodes and 29,347 candidate sensor nodes.

Two ways to represent settlement

  • Method A, local soil softening: the springs under selected footings are scaled down by a factor α from 0.9 to 0.05. Five families of footing sets give 10,368 scenarios.
  • Method B, imposed ground movement: three unit cases per footing (uniform, linear in x, linear in y), 5,184 load cases solved in one step per building. Any settlement field up to 20 mm is a linear combination of them.

Verification

A dataset of 10,692 analyses is only useful if each one is right. Three layers of checking:

  • Eight gates per building: total mass, centre of mass, soil reaction, restraint reaction, tension, solver messages, bearing pressure and symmetry. All 108 buildings passed; the symmetry gate applies to the 99 symmetric ones.
  • Superposition: method B combinations match independent analyses in all 108 buildings, with a residual of at most 6.6×10−7 against a tolerance of 1×10−6.
  • Mesh convergence: three buildings re-solved at two and four times the element divisions. Candidate tilts change by at most 0.789 % (limit 2 %) and their ranking holds (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.99975).

The dataset

432 files, four per building: node features including footing stiffness, scenario information, method A labels and method B labels, with the displacement and rotation at every candidate node. The tilt label is the scenario minus the reference state (method A) or the perturbation response (method B).

GNN Studio 1.0

To make the pipeline usable by the next researcher, I packaged it as an installable Windows program. Every pipeline step, the mesh study and adding new buildings are available from the GUI. Pre-run checks catch problems before a job is submitted, and safeguards prevent the baseline results from being overwritten. The program drives an existing Abaqus installation rather than bundling it.

Limits and next steps

The dataset uses one soil stiffness and linear-elastic service analysis, and the mesh study does not cover the largest buildings. The detection threshold and the utility function for choosing a location are still to be set; training the graph neural network, optimising the location and defining field criteria are the next stage of the project.
